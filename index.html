    How to read this dashboard

    A plain-language guide to what each block shows and how the numbers are worked out.

    What time period do Trending Topics and Top Keywords cover?
    They look only at the stories currently loaded from the news feeds. Each outlet's feed lists its most recent stories, so a busy outlet covers a shorter stretch of time than a quiet one. The date filters in the filter menu narrow this further, and clicking Fetch or Refresh brings in newer stories. Nothing older than what the feeds still list is included.

    Fetch / Refresh
    Nothing loads until you click it (filter icon, top right). It reads every outlet's feed and checks where each story ranks on Google News. It shows "Fetch" first, and after a filter change. It shows "Refresh" when data is already on screen.
    The four small numbers
    How many DH stories and competitor stories are loaded, how many topic groups they form, and how many coverage gaps there are (topics competitors have that DH does not).
    Top Keywords
    The words that appear most often in headlines and summaries. The number is how many stories use that word. Hover a word to see those stories, and click one to open it.
    Trending Topics
    Stories that share at least two key words are grouped as one topic. The biggest groups are the trending ones. The chips show which outlets covered it, and how many stories are DH's and how many are competitors'. Hover any chip to see the stories.
    Competitor Coverage
    Compares outlets topic by topic. Gap means competitors have the story and DH does not. Covered means DH and a competitor both have it. DH exclusive means only DH has it. The number in a box is how many stories that outlet has on the topic. G#3 means its best Google News position was 3rd. Use the dropdown to switch between top trending topics and all topics.
    How Google News ranking works here
    • What is checked: when you click Fetch, the dashboard asks Google News (India, English) for its Top stories, for a "Karnataka or Bengaluru" search from the last day, and for one search per each of the 12 biggest topics (their three main words, last two days). That is up to 14 lookups.
    • What "rank" means: the position of a story in the list Google returns, in Google's own order. #1 is the top result, so a lower number is better. Only the first results Google returns (up to about 100) are looked at.
    • How a story is matched: a story counts as found when the same outlet appears in the Google list with a headline that shares most of its key words (at least 60%). If it shows up in several lookups, its best (lowest) position is kept.
    • Where you see it: a blue Google News #n badge on a story card (hover it to see which lookup found it); a small G#n under an outlet's number in Competitor Coverage, which is the best position among that outlet's stories on the topic; and a summary line there such as "DH 5/35 stories ranked", meaning 5 of 35 DH stories were found.
    • What it does not mean: it is the position in these searches, not a guaranteed place on the Google News home page. Google personalises results, so your own view can differ. No badge does not mean the story is absent from Google, only that it was not in the results we asked for. Stories outside the 12 biggest topics are less likely to be found.
    • How fresh it is: ranks are saved with the data and stay as they were until the next Fetch or Refresh. If Google News cannot be reached, the Feed Status block warns you and no badges are shown.
    Latest Stories
    The newest headlines. The blue badge shows its Google News position when it was found. "Read more" shows the text the feed provides, which is sometimes only a short summary. "Open in browser" goes to the full article.
