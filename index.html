    How to read this dashboard

    A plain-language guide to what each block shows and how the numbers are worked out.

    What time period do Trending Topics and Top Keywords cover?
    They use only the stories published inside the time window you choose in the filter menu: the last 24 hours by default, or the last 48 hours or 7 days. The same window applies to every outlet, so they are compared fairly. The From and To date filters narrow it further, and clicking Fetch or Refresh brings in newer stories. A feed only lists an outlet's most recent stories, so a very busy outlet may not fill a long window.
    Who is compared?
    Our paper:
    Competitors: . Anything labelled "competitor" in this dashboard means one of these outlets.
    Where their stories come from: each outlet's feed covers all kinds of stories, not one section. Livemint and News18 have no single all-sections feed, so several of their section feeds are combined. Moneycontrol's own feeds are out of date, so its stories are read from a Google News search, and its links are Google News redirect links. The Feed Status block shows which feeds are working.
    Fetch / Refresh
    On the published website the latest collected data is shown as soon as you open the page, and the time it was collected is shown at the top. When you run the dashboard on your own computer, nothing loads until you click it (filter icon, top right). It reads every outlet's feed, checks where each story ranks on Google News, and checks Google Trends for matching searches. It shows "Fetch" first, and again after you change a filter or the time window. It shows "Refresh" when data is already on screen. While it works, the button shows a spinner. On the published website, browsers are not allowed to read the news sites directly, so a scheduled job on GitHub collects everything about every 30 minutes and Fetch or Refresh loads that latest collection. The status line then shows when it was collected.
    The four small numbers
    How many DH stories and competitor stories are inside the chosen time window, how many topic groups they form, and how many coverage gaps there are (topics competitors have that DH does not).
    Top Keywords
    The words that appear most often in headlines and summaries. The number is how many stories use that word. Hover a word to see those stories, and click one to open it.
    Trending Topics
    Stories that share at least two key words are grouped as one topic. The biggest groups are the trending ones. Each row has three columns: the topic (with its main words underneath), the stories (the total, how many are DH's and how many are competitors'), and the competitor outlets that covered it. Hover a count or an outlet name to see its stories, and click a headline to open it.
    Competitor Coverage
    Compares outlets topic by topic, one column per outlet. Gap means competitors have the story and DH does not. Covered means DH and a competitor both have it. DH exclusive means only DH has it. The number in a box is how many stories that outlet has on the topic; hover it, or the status label, to see those stories and click one to open it. G#3 means its best Google News position was 3rd. The dropdown on the left switches between top trending topics (the default), all topics, or everything except the top trending ones, and the buttons beside it filter by status. When the table is wider than the screen, scroll sideways: the Topic column stays in view. The copy icon at the end of each row copies a ready-made prompt plus the story links, grouped under each outlet's name, so you can paste it into an AI tool to compare the stories and get ideas for making DH's version stronger.
    How Google News ranking works here
    • What is checked: when you click Fetch, the dashboard asks Google News (India, English) for its Top stories, for a "Karnataka or Bengaluru" search from the last day, and for one search per each of the 12 biggest topics (their three main words, last day). That is up to 14 lookups.
    • What "rank" means: the position of a story in the list Google returns, in Google's own order. #1 is the top result, so a lower number is better. Only the first results Google returns (up to about 100) are looked at.
    • How a story is matched: a story counts as found when the same outlet appears in the Google list with a headline that shares most of its key words (at least 60%). If it shows up in several lookups, its best (lowest) position is kept.
    • Where you see it: a blue Google News #n badge on a story card (hover it to see which lookup found it); a small G#n under an outlet's number in Competitor Coverage, which is the best position among that outlet's stories on the topic; and a summary line there such as "DH 5/35 stories ranked", meaning 5 of 35 DH stories were found.
    • What it does not mean: it is the position in these searches, not a guaranteed place on the Google News home page. Google personalises results, so your own view can differ. No badge does not mean the story is absent from Google, only that it was not in the results we asked for. Stories outside the 12 biggest topics are less likely to be found.
    • How fresh it is: ranks are saved with the data and stay as they were until the next Fetch or Refresh. If Google News cannot be reached, the Feed Status block warns you and no badges are shown.
    Google Trends: "Trending" search badges
    • What it is: Google Trends lists what people in India are searching for right now. That is different from Google News, which ranks published articles. Trends shows interest, News shows placement.
    • How a story is marked: on Fetch, the dashboard reads Google's current "Trending now" searches for India. Under each trending search, Google lists a few news items about it. A story gets a purple Trending: phrase badge only when it is the same story as one of those listed news items (same headline wording, not just a shared word). The number (for example 200+) is Google's approximate search volume, a rough range, not an exact count.
    • In Competitor Coverage: a purple Trending search tag next to a topic means at least one story in that topic matched a trending search. Hover it to see the phrases.
    • Limits: Google lists only about ten trending searches at a time, so only a few stories match on any refresh. A story that only mentions the search phrase, but is not one of the news items Google lists, is not marked. Headline matching compares wording, so an occasional match can be loose. A story with no badge is not necessarily uninteresting. Google Search rankings and Google Discover are not tracked.
    Feed Status
    The block above this guide. A red or amber banner says in plain words when a feed is blocked, rate-limited or not loading, or when Google News or Google Trends could not be reached. Each outlet's row shows whether it is working and the time span its feed covers. Stories from a feed that is not working are missing from every number on the page.
    Latest Stories
    Every story inside the chosen time window, newest first by default, each with a small thumbnail beside its outlet and time. Use the Outlet, Show and Sort by dropdowns to narrow or reorder the list, and the page buttons to move through it (the Show dropdown can list only the stories matched to a Google Trends search). An orange In a top topic label means the story belongs to one of the biggest topic groups shown in Trending Topics (hover it to see which). It describes the topic, not how many people are reading the story. The blue badge shows its Google News position when it was found. "Read more" shows the text the feed provides, which is sometimes only a short summary. "Open in browser" goes to the full article.
